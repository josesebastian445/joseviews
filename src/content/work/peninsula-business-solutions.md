## The situation

A portfolio of WordPress and WooCommerce sites spread across GoDaddy, Bluehost,
AWS and Hetzner, serving clients in several time zones. Store downtime meant lost
revenue immediately, and the same person handling that was also the first line for
every laptop, mailbox and VPN problem in the business.

## What I did

**Stabilised the stores first.** Uptime monitoring, off-site backups, and staging
environments so plugin updates stopped being a live experiment.

**Built a repeatable SEO process.** Keyword research, on-page optimisation, and
technical fixes tracked in a shared sheet with owners and dates — not a one-off
audit that gets read once and filed.

**Ran paid alongside organic.** Google Ads and Facebook campaigns with keyword
analysis and monthly analytics reporting, so we could see which channel deserved
the next dirham.

**Kept the infrastructure boring.** Office 365 administration, FortiGate firewall
rules, server and network maintenance, and remote support for staff across
locations.

**Trained the juniors.** Documented the process so the work did not depend on any
one person being reachable.

## The result

The lasting change was moving from reactive to scheduled. Updates, backups and
reporting happened on a calendar rather than in response to a complaint, and the
stores stopped being a source of weekend emergencies.

Splitting the SEO work into technical, on-page and off-page — each with an owner
and a date — meant progress was visible between reports instead of arriving as a
surprise at the end of a quarter.
